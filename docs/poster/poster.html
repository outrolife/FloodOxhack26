River Clock

Water upstream is Oxford's future. We read it as a clock.

When, and how likely, will five Oxford trouble spots flood in the next 72 h?

The finding: the flood is already on its way

Median time for a flood's half-rise to travel between gauges. We only use gauge pairs seen in 3 or more past floods (9 of 11). On the Thames the peaks are slower: Cassington to New Botley, 32 h peak to peak.

How it works

1. ReadEA river levels upstream, every 15 min

2. Timehow long past floods took to reach Oxford

3. ForecastOxford levels 6–72 h ahead (LightGBM)

4. Decideact when the chance beats cost ÷ loss

On the laptop, one screen: the river map with a clock per spot, a timeline of our forecast against the real level (River Clock warned · EA warned · Flooded), and a switch between today and past floods.

Under 30%

2026 near miss: no flood came

The river came within 11 cm of Botley’s flood line. Our highest chance was just under 30%, below every spot’s alarm level, so no false alarm.

What it can't do

  • Few floods: 7 events since 2007, so uncertainty is wide.
  • No future rain: only water already in the river. River flooding only. Replays may have seen later floods.
  • Stand-ins: Osney uses a gauge 1.2 km west. Wolvercote has no forecast; Kings Mill is offline live.
  • Next: rain forecasts lifted 72 h accuracy from 11% to 28% in a test, but added 3 false alarms, so they’re off.
